# Problem.

A pond contains one amoeba. Every minute it dies, stays as it is, splits into two, or splits into three, the four outcomes being equally likely. Its offspring behave the same way and independently of one another. What is the probability that the population dies out?

# Solution.

The probability is $\sqrt2 - 1 \approx 0.4142$.

### The process

Let $\xi$ be the number of individuals that one individual leaves behind after one step, with $\mathbb P(\xi = k) = p_k$, and let

$$
\phi(s) = \mathbb E\left[s^{\xi}\right] = \sum_{k\ge0}p_k s^{k}, \qquad s \in [0,1] .
$$

Set $Z_0 = 1$ and, given $Z_n$,

$$
Z_{n+1} = \sum_{j=1}^{Z_n}\xi_{n,j} ,
$$

where the $\xi_{n,j}$ are independent copies of $\xi$, independent of $Z_0, \dots, Z_n$. Extinction is the event $\bigcup_{n\ge0}\{Z_n = 0\}$ and $q$ denotes its probability.

### The extinction probability is the smallest fixed point of $\phi$

**Two properties of $\phi$.** For $s \in [0,1]$ the series is dominated termwise by $\sum_k p_k = 1$, so $\phi$ is well defined, and if $s_m \to s$ in $[0,1]$ then dominated convergence gives $\phi(s_m) \to \phi(s)$. In other words, $\phi$ is continuous on $[0,1]$. It is also non-decreasing with $\phi(1) = 1$, which implies that a fixed point in $[0,1]$ exists.

**The generating functions compose.** Let $f_n(s) = \mathbb E\left[s^{Z_n}\right]$. Conditionally on $Z_n = k$ the sum defining $Z_{n+1}$ has $k$ independent terms distributed as $\xi$, so

$$
\mathbb E\left[s^{Z_{n+1}} \,\middle|\, Z_n = k\right] = \phi(s)^{k} ,
$$

and averaging over $Z_n$ gives $f_{n+1}(s) = f_n\big(\phi(s)\big)$. With $f_0(s) = s$, induction yields $f_n = \phi^{\circ n}$, and

$$
f_{n+1} = \phi \circ f_n .
$$

We also have

$$
f_{n + 1}(s) 
= \mathbb E\left[ \mathbb E [s^{Z_{n+1}} \mid Z_1] \right]
= \mathbb E\left[ \mathbb E [s^{Z_{n}}]^{Z_1} \right]
= \phi(f_n(s)),
$$

i.e., 

$$
f_{n+1} = \phi \circ f_n.
$$

**The finite-time probabilities increase to $q$.** Put $q_n = \mathbb P(Z_n = 0) = f_n(0)$ with $q_0 = 0$. The events $\{Z_n = 0\}$ increase in $n$ and their union is extinction. Continuity of $\mathbb P$ from below gives $q_n \uparrow q$.

**$q$ is a fixed point.** Evaluating $f_{n+1} = \phi \circ f_n$ at $0$ gives 

$$
q_{n+1} = f_{n+1}(0) = \phi(f_n(0)) = \phi(q_n).
$$ 

Letting $n \to \infty$, the left side tends to $q$ and, $\phi$ being continuous, the right side tends to $\phi(q)$, so $q = \phi(q)$.

**It is the smallest.** Let $s$ be any fixed point in $[0,1]$ with $q_0 = 0 \le s$, and if $q_n \le s$ then $q_{n+1} = \phi(q_n) \le \phi(s) = s$ because $\phi$ is non-decreasing. By induction $q_n \le s$ for every $n$, hence $q = \lim_n q_n \le s$.

### Applying it

The amoeba population is such a process, with $p_0 = p_1 = p_2 = p_3 = \tfrac14$ and

$$
\phi(s) = \tfrac14\left(1 + s + s^{2} + s^{3}\right) .
$$

Its extinction probability is therefore the smallest fixed point of $\phi$ in $[0,1]$. Solving,

$$
\tfrac14\left(1+s+s^{2}+s^{3}\right) = s
\iff s^{3}+s^{2}-3s+1 = 0
\iff (s-1)\left(s^{2}+2s-1\right) = 0 ,
$$

whose roots are $1$ and $-1 \pm \sqrt2$. The two in $[0,1]$ are $\sqrt2 - 1$ and $1$, so

$$
q = \sqrt2 - 1 .
$$
