# Problem.

Fifty-two cards are dealt at random to four players, thirteen each. What is the probability that every player receives an ace?

# Solution.


### Method 1: Place the aces one at a time

Regard the deal as fifty-two labelled seats, thirteen belonging to each player, into which the cards fall. The four aces then occupy four seats chosen uniformly at random among all sets of four, and the question is whether those seats belong to four different players.

Seat the aces in turn. The first goes anywhere. Of the fifty-one seats left, the thirty-nine outside the first ace's player are the ones that keep the count going; then thirty-eight are barred and twenty-six of the remaining fifty are good; then only the thirteen seats of the last player will do, out of forty-nine. Hence

$$
P = \frac{39}{51}\cdot\frac{26}{50}\cdot\frac{13}{49} = \frac{2197}{20825} .
$$

### Method 2: Count the deals

A deal assigns the fifty-two cards to four distinguishable hands of thirteen, and there are $\dfrac{52!}{(13!)^{4}}$ of them. A favourable deal is built by handing the four aces to the four players, in one of $4!$ ways, and splitting the forty-eight other cards into four groups of twelve, in one of $\dfrac{48!}{(12!)^{4}}$ ways. So

$$
P = \frac{4!\,\dfrac{48!}{(12!)^{4}}}{\dfrac{52!}{(13!)^{4}}} = 4!\cdot\frac{48!}{52!}\left(\frac{13!}{12!}\right)^{4} = \frac{24 \cdot 13^{4}}{52\cdot51\cdot50\cdot49} = \frac{2197}{20825} .
$$
