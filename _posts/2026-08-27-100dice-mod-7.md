# Problem. 
One hundred fair dice are rolled. What is the probability that their sum is divisible by $7$?

# Answer.

Let $\{X_n\}$ be i.i.d. uniform on $\{1, \dots, 6\}$ and write $S_n = X_1 + \cdots + X_n$.

### Method 1: Symmetry and a one-step recursion

Let $a_n = \mathbb{P}(S_n \equiv 0 \bmod 7)$. We have

$$
\begin{aligned}
a_{n+1} 
&= \mathbb{P}(S_n + X_{n+1} \equiv 0 \bmod 7) \\
&= \sum_{i=1}^6 \mathbb P (X_{n+1} = i)\mathbb P(S_n \equiv 7-i \bmod 7)\\
&= \frac16 (1 - \mathbb P(S_n \equiv 0 \bmod 7))\\
&= \frac16 (1 - a_n)
\end{aligned}
$$

The map has fixed point $1/7$, and subtracting it linearises the recursion,

$$
a_{n+1} - \frac{1}{7} = -\frac{1}{6}\left(a_n - \frac{1}{7}\right).
$$

Since $a_1 = 0$, iterating gives

$$
a_n = \frac{1}{7}\left(1 + (-1)^n 6^{\,1-n}\right),
$$

and at $n = 100$ this is $\frac{1}{7}\left(1 + 6^{-99}\right)$.

### Method 2: Markov Chain

$S_n \bmod 7$ is a random walk on $\{0, 1, \cdots, 6\}$ whose step is uniform on the nonzero elements. Its transition matrix is

$$
P = \frac{J - I}{6},
$$

where $J = \mathbf 1 \mathbf 1^\top$ is the all-ones matrix. Now $J$ has eigenvalue $7$ on vector $\mathbf 1$ and $0$ on its orthogonal complement, so $P$ has eigenvalue $\frac{7-1}{6} = 1$ on $\mathbf 1$ and $\frac{0-1}{6} = -\frac{1}{6}$ on the six-dimensional complement.

Split the initial distribution $\delta_0$ into its uniform part and the rest, $\delta_0 = \frac17\mathbf 1 + (\delta_0 - \frac17\mathbf 1)$. The first piece is fixed by $P$ and the second is scaled by $-1/6$ at every step, i.e.,

$$
\delta_0 P^n = \frac17\mathbf 1 + \left(-\frac{1}{6}\right)^n \left(\delta_0 - \frac17\mathbf 1\right).
$$

Reading off the coordinate at $0$,

$$
a_n = \frac{1}{7}\left(1 + 6\left(-\frac{1}{6}\right)^{n}\right).
$$
