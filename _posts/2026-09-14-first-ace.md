# Problem.

A standard deck of $52$ cards is shuffled and dealt one card at a time. On average, how many cards do we turn over until the first ace shows up?

# Solution.

Write $n = 52$ for the number of cards and $a = 4$ for the number of aces; the other $n - a = 48$ cards we call **blanks**. Let $T$ be the position of the first ace. We keep $n$ and $a$ general and plug in the numbers at the end.

The deck is uniformly shuffled: every one of the $n!$ orderings is equally likely.

### Method 1: One indicator per blank

The $T - 1$ cards above the first ace are exactly the blanks that come before **all** the aces. So

$$
T = 1 + \sum_{b} I_b ,
$$

where the sum runs over the $n - a$ blanks and $I_b$ is the indicator that blank $b$ is dealt before every ace.

Fix a blank $b$ and look only at the $a + 1$ cards consisting of $b$ and the $a$ aces. Shuffling these $a + 1$ cards among the slots they occupy, and leaving every other card where it is, maps deck orderings to deck orderings bijectively. So all $(a + 1)!$ relative orders of these $a + 1$ cards are equally likely, and $b$ is the first of them with probability

$$
\mathbb{P}(I_b = 1) = \frac{1}{a + 1} .
$$

By linearity of expectation, whether or not the $I_b$ are independent (they are not),

$$
\mathbb{E}[T] = 1 + \frac{n - a}{a + 1} = \frac{n + 1}{a + 1} .
$$


### Method 2: The gaps are exchangeable

The $a$ aces cut the blanks into $a + 1$ **gaps**: $G_0$ blanks before the first ace, $G_1$ between the first and second ace, and so on, up to $G_a$ after the last ace. Then

$$
G_0 + G_1 + \cdots + G_a = n - a, \qquad T = G_0 + 1 .
$$

The claim is that all $a + 1$ gaps have the same distribution. A vector $(g_0, \dots, g_a)$ of nonnegative integers summing to $n - a$ corresponds to exactly one set of ace positions: the first ace sits at $g_0 + 1$, the second at $g_0 + g_1 + 2$, and so on. Each set of ace positions is realised by the same number $a! \, (n - a)!$ of deck orderings, since the aces can be arranged in their slots in $a!$ ways and the blanks in theirs in $(n - a)!$ ways. So every gap vector is equally likely. Permuting the coordinates of a gap vector gives another gap vector with the same sum, so the joint law of $(G_0, \dots, G_a)$ does not change when the gaps are permuted: the gaps are **exchangeable**, and in particular $\mathbb{E}[G_0] = \mathbb{E}[G_1] = \cdots = \mathbb{E}[G_a]$.

Taking expectations in the sum,

$$
(a + 1) \, \mathbb{E}[G_0] = n - a, \qquad \mathbb{E}[T] = \frac{n - a}{a + 1} + 1 = \frac{n + 1}{a + 1} .
$$

### Method 3: Sum the tail probabilities

For an integer-valued $T \geq 0$ we have $T = \sum_{k \geq 0} \mathbf{1}\{T > k\}$, since the indicator equals $1$ exactly for $k = 0, 1, \dots, T - 1$. Taking expectations,

$$
\mathbb{E}[T] = \sum_{k \geq 0} \mathbb{P}(T > k) .
$$

Now $T > k$ means the top $k$ cards are all blanks. The top $k$ cards form a uniformly random $k$-subset of the deck, so for $0 \leq k \leq n - a$

$$
\mathbb{P}(T > k) = \frac{\binom{n - a}{k}}{\binom{n}{k}} = \frac{(n - a)! \, (n - k)!}{n! \, (n - a - k)!} = \frac{\binom{n - k}{a}}{\binom{n}{a}} ,
$$

and $\mathbb{P}(T > k) = 0$ for larger $k$. The right-hand form has its own meaning: the $a$ ace positions are a uniform $a$-subset of the $n$ slots, and $T > k$ says they all fall in the last $n - k$ slots.

Summing,

$$
\mathbb{E}[T] = \frac{1}{\binom{n}{a}} \sum_{k = 0}^{n - a} \binom{n - k}{a} = \frac{1}{\binom{n}{a}} \sum_{j = a}^{n} \binom{j}{a} = \frac{\binom{n + 1}{a + 1}}{\binom{n}{a}} .
$$

The last step is the **hockey-stick** identity: an $(a + 1)$-subset of $\{1, \dots, n + 1\}$ whose largest element is $j + 1$ is determined by its other $a$ elements, chosen from $\{1, \dots, j\}$, so grouping the subsets by their largest element gives $\sum_{j = a}^{n} \binom{j}{a} = \binom{n + 1}{a + 1}$. Finally

$$
\frac{\binom{n + 1}{a + 1}}{\binom{n}{a}} = \frac{(n + 1)!}{(a + 1)! \, (n - a)!} \cdot \frac{a! \, (n - a)!}{n!} = \frac{n + 1}{a + 1} .
$$

### Method 4: Condition on the top card

Write $E(n, a)$ for the expected position of the first ace in a deck of $n$ cards with $a \geq 1$ aces. Turn over the top card.

- With probability $\frac{a}{n}$ it is an ace, and $T = 1$.
- With probability $\frac{n - a}{n}$ it is a blank. The remaining $n - 1$ cards are in uniformly random order and still contain all $a$ aces, so from here on it is the same game with $n - 1$ cards: $T = 1 + T'$ with $\mathbb{E}[T'] = E(n - 1, a)$.

Hence

$$
E(n, a) = 1 + \frac{n - a}{n} \, E(n - 1, a), \qquad E(a, a) = 1 .
$$

Small cases suggest the answer: $E(a, a) = 1$, $E(a + 1, a) = 1 + \frac{1}{a + 1}$, and 

$$
E(a + 2, a) = 1 + \frac{2}{a + 2} \cdot \frac{a + 2}{a + 1} = 1 + \frac{2}{a + 1},
$$

which points to $E(n, a) = \frac{n + 1}{a + 1}$. Induction on $n$ confirms it. The base case $n = a$ gives $\frac{a + 1}{a + 1} = 1$, and if $E(n - 1, a) = \frac{n}{a + 1}$ then

$$
E(n, a) = 1 + \frac{n - a}{n} \cdot \frac{n}{a + 1} = \frac{(a + 1) + (n - a)}{a + 1} = \frac{n + 1}{a + 1} .
$$

With $n = 52$ and $a = 4$,

$$
\mathbb{E}[T] = \frac{53}{5}.
$$

# Remark.

The **tail-sum formula**: for a random variable $T$ taking values in $\{0, 1, 2, \dots\}$,

$$
\mathbb{E}[T] = \sum_{k = 0}^{\infty} \mathbb{P}(T > k) = \sum_{k = 1}^{\infty} \mathbb{P}(T \geq k) .
$$

The two sums are the same sum written twice, since $\{T > k\} = \{T \geq k + 1\}$; shifting the index $k \mapsto k + 1$ turns one into the other. So it suffices to prove the second form.

**Proof by indicators.** For every outcome, $T$ is the number of positive integers $k$ with $k \leq T$, that is

$$
T = \sum_{k = 1}^{\infty} \mathbf{1}\{T \geq k\}.
$$

Taking expectations term by term,

$$
\mathbb{E}[T] = \sum_{k = 1}^{\infty} \mathbb{E}\big[\mathbf{1}\{T \geq k\}\big] = \sum_{k = 1}^{\infty} \mathbb{P}(T \geq k) .
$$
