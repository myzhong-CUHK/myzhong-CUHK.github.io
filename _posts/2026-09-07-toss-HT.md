# Problem.

Two players toss a fair coin alternately, $A$ first. The game stops the first time a head is immediately followed by a tail, and whoever tossed that tail wins. What is the probability that $A$ wins?

# Solution.

$A$ wins with probability $\tfrac49$.

### Method 1: Condition on the first toss

Since the game ends almost surely with exactly one winner, $P(B) = 1 - P(A)$. Split on $A$'s opening toss,

$$
P(A) = \tfrac12 P(A \mid H) + \tfrac12 P(A \mid T) .
$$

**The tail case.** A tail cannot start an $HT$, and there is no head before it to complete one, so an opening tail is as if the toss never happened and only the turn has changed. What follows is a fresh game with $B$ tossing first, which puts $B$ in exactly the position $A$ held at the start. So $A$ is now the second player, and

$$
P(A \mid T) = 1 - P(A) .
$$

**The head case.** After $A$'s head it is $B$'s turn with a head pending, and $A$'s chance from there is $P(A \mid H)$, so $B$'s is $1 - P(A \mid H)$. Split on $B$'s reply. If $B$ tosses a tail, which happens half the time, $B$ wins at once and $A$ gets nothing. If $B$ tosses a head, then $A$ is to toss with a head pending, which is precisely the position $B$ occupied a moment ago with the names exchanged, so $A$ now has what $B$ had, namely $1 - P(A \mid H)$. Hence

$$
P(A \mid H) = \frac12 \cdot 0 + \frac12\left(1 - P(A \mid H)\right) \implies P(A \mid H) = \frac13 .
$$

**Combining.** Substituting both into the split,

$$
P(A) = \frac12 \cdot \frac13 + \frac12\left(1 - P(A)\right) \implies P(A) = \frac49 .
$$

### Method 2: Sum over the winning toss

Until the game ends the record must read

$$
\underbrace{T \cdots T}_{a \ \ge\  0} \ \underbrace{H \cdots H}_{b \ \ge\  1} \ T ,
$$

because once a head appears the next tail ends the game, so every tail before the last one precedes every head. The winning toss is number $a+b+1$, and $A$ makes it exactly when $a+b$ is even.

Each such record has probability $2^{-(a+b+1)}$. Writing $k = a+b$, the pairs with $a \ge 0$, $b \ge 1$ and $a+b=k$ number $k$, so the game ends on toss $k+1$ with probability $k\,2^{-(k+1)}$, and $\sum_{k\ge1} k\,2^{-(k+1)} = 1$ as it must. Keeping the even $k$ and writing $k = 2j$,

$$
P(A) = \sum_{k \text{ even}} \frac{k}{2^{\,k+1}} = \frac12 \sum_{j\ge1} 2j \left(\frac14\right)^{j} = \frac12 \cdot \frac{2 \cdot \frac14}{\left(1-\frac14\right)^{2}} = \frac49 .
$$

This route needs no symmetry argument and yields the whole distribution of the ending time along the way.
