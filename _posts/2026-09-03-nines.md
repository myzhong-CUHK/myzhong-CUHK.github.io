# Problem.

What is the $100$th digit to the right of the decimal point in the decimal representation of $\left(1+\sqrt2\right)^{3000}$?

# Solution.

The digit is $9$.

### Method 1: Add the conjugate

Apply the binomial theorem to $\left(1+\sqrt2\right)^{n}$ and split the sum according to the parity of $k$:

$$
\left(1+\sqrt2\right)^{n} = \sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}1^{\,n-k}\sqrt2^{\,k}
= \sum_{\substack{k=2j \\ 0\le j\le n/2}}\binom{n}{k}1^{\,n-k}\sqrt2^{\,k}
+ \sum_{\substack{k=2j+1 \\ 0\le j< n/2}}\binom{n}{k}1^{\,n-k}\sqrt2^{\,k} .
$$

The expansion of $\left(1-\sqrt2\right)^{n}$ has exactly the same terms with $\left(-\sqrt2\right)^{k}$ in place of $\sqrt2^{\,k}$, so the even part is unchanged and the odd part flips sign:

$$
\left(1-\sqrt2\right)^{n} = \sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}1^{\,n-k}\left(-\sqrt2\right)^{k}
= \sum_{\substack{k=2j \\ 0\le j\le n/2}}\binom{n}{k}1^{\,n-k}\sqrt2^{\,k}
- \sum_{\substack{k=2j+1 \\ 0\le j< n/2}}\binom{n}{k}1^{\,n-k}\sqrt2^{\,k} .
$$

Adding the two lines cancels the odd sums and doubles the even one, so

$$
S_n = \left(1+\sqrt2\right)^{n} + \left(1-\sqrt2\right)^{n} = 2\sum_{\substack{k=2j \\ 0\le j\le n/2}}\binom{n}{k}1^{\,n-k}\sqrt2^{\,k},
$$

which is always an integer, because $\sqrt2^{\,k}$ is a power of $2$ whenever $k$ is even.

Now $1-\sqrt2 \approx -0.4142$ has absolute value below $1$, and $3000$ is **even**, so

$$
\varepsilon := \left(1-\sqrt2\right)^{3000} = \left(\sqrt2-1\right)^{3000} > 0
$$

is a tiny positive number, and

$$
\left(1+\sqrt2\right)^{3000} = S_{3000} - \varepsilon .
$$

Since $0 < \varepsilon < 1$, the integer part of $\left(1+\sqrt2\right)^{3000} = S_{3000} - \varepsilon$ is $S_{3000}-1$, and the fractional part is $1-\varepsilon$.

Everything therefore hinges on the size of $\varepsilon$. Once $\varepsilon < 10^{-m}$,

$$
0.\underbrace{99\cdots9}_{m} < 1 - \varepsilon < 1.
$$

#### How small is $\left(1-\sqrt2\right)^{3000}$
Since $\sqrt2 - 1 = 0.414\ldots < \tfrac12$, we get $\varepsilon < 2^{-3000}$. Now $2^{10} = 1024 > 10^{3}$, so $2^{3000} = \left(2^{10}\right)^{300} > 10^{900}$ and

$$
\varepsilon < 10^{-900} .
$$
